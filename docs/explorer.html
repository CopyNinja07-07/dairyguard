The heat test simulated

Growing bacteria give off a tiny amount of heat. Put a spoonful of milk in a calorimeter at 20 °C and time how soon the heat shows up. Busy bacteria show up sooner. That one number tells us what the thermometer can't.

Temperature log only–

Gives one answer for every carton on the truck, because every carton has the same log.

Log + heat test–

Adds the heat test of each batch. Now each carton gets its own answer.

Green: the answer was right for that carton. Red: wrong. The heat test is simulated with a 5% measurement error. Over 48,000 simulated trips it wins back 69–94% of the accuracy the log alone loses. It has not yet been tried on real milk.

Fine print

What this doesn't tell you.

  • The trucks, fridges and heat tests are simulated from published experiments. Real delivery logs, real heat tests on milk and new lab counts are the next step.
  • "Crossing the limit" means the bacteria count passed the FSSAI hygiene limit for pasteurised milk (or, for Listeria, grew tenfold). It is a count, not a taste test and not a safety verdict. If milk smells or looks off, don't drink it.
  • About half of the simulated trips cross the limit by design, so these percentages are not real-world rates.
