Why a second model

Kinetic showed where it stops.

DairyGuard Kinetic treats every batch of milk as a draw from one fixed population. Its own results say that is now the limiting assumption.

Prototype

Already running, on simulated batches.

Layers 1–4 and training stages 0–2 are written and tested in experiments/batch_prototype.py. It learns from 30 simulated batches and judges 200 new ones on one trip. The batches and the link between day-one tests and biology are invented, so this tests the code, not milk.

simulation87–100%

True batch parameters inside their 90% intervals after fitting (stage 0).

simulation16–32 h

Median error of hours left using the population only, as DairyGuard Kinetic does.

simulation14–15 h

Median error with day-one tests, across four random seeds.

simulation0.82–0.98

How often the cautious value was safe with tests. Below 0.85 fails the blueprint's test: more batches needed.

Fixed in advance

It replaces Kinetic only if it passes.

On locked test batches, compared with DairyGuard Kinetic, a classical Baranyi fit, and a black-box recurrent network of similar size.

Cuts the median error of hours left at 4 °C by at least 30%.

Keeps its 90% uncertainty band honest: coverage between 85 and 95%.

Is no worse than Kinetic at telling crossed cartons from safe ones (AUC).

Beats the black-box network on error of log counts.

If it fails, the failure is reported in full. A negative answer is a result.
