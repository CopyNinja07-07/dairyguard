The case

Every carton has two histories.

The temperature it went through, and the bacteria it started with. A logger records the first. Nobody records the second. So how well can a temperature log really tell if milk is still good?

01 · THE ROUTE

Milk travels

Dairy, truck, depot, shop, home. At every hand-off a door opens, a cooler struggles, a crate waits in the sun.

02 · THE WITNESS

A logger watches

Cheap temperature loggers now ride with the milk. They record every warm minute faithfully.

03 · THE BLIND SPOT

Bacteria hide

How many bacteria were in the milk, which kind, and how fast they wake up. No thermometer can see any of it.

What we found

The thermometer isn't the problem. The hidden biology is.

We scored every way of reading a temperature log on one question: can it tell the cartons that will cross the bacteria limit from the ones that won't? A coin flip scores 50. A perfect method scores 100.

Lost because no one can see the bacteria simulated27–40 pts
Won back by one heat test of the milk simulated69–94% of that
Gained by a growth formula over a simple degree-hour count≤ 4 pts
Gained by a lab-grade thermometer≤ 2 pts
Lost by reading the temperature hourly, not every minuteabout 0

From 240,000 simulated deliveries in five different worlds, built from published experiments. The heat test is a calorimetry measurement; it has been simulated, not yet done on real milk. "Crossing the limit" means the bacteria count passed the FSSAI hygiene limit for pasteurised milk. It is a count, not a taste test.

How we know

An exam where we hold the answer key.

In real life no one counts the bacteria in every carton, so a prediction can never be checked. So we built a virtual cold chain where the true answer is always known, tied every number to real experiments, and checked it against real data.

Read the scienceGrowth laws from predictive microbiology, FSSAI milk limits, 64 published sources.
Gather real data373 bacterial growth records in milk, compiled from the public ComBase database.
Build the world240,000 simulated deliveries, each tracked minute by minute.
Check itPredicts a published lab experiment within 4%, and held-out Listeria data closely.
Prove the mathsExact results explain every number before the simulation runs.
The key idea

Bacteria keep their own clock.

Milk at 4, 8 and 12 °C spoils at very different times. But switch to the bacteria's own clock, which runs faster when it's warm, and the three curves become one.

This is the mathematical heart of the project. The whole temperature history matters only through one running total, the "biological time". That explains why a cheap thermometer is good enough, why hourly readings are enough, and why the hidden biology is what's left. The same idea, called generalized time, has been used in thermal analysis for sixty years.

The models

One model today. A smarter one next.

Today · DairyGuard Kinetic

Reads the log

Turns a temperature log into "how much of its life has this milk used up", and a probability that it crossed the limit.

  • Built on proven growth equations and the biological clock
  • Small enough to run on a cheap logger chip
  • Checked against real experiments and ComBase data
  • Its limit: it has to assume the biology of each batch
In development · DairyGuard Neural

Learns the biology

Keeps the proven maths and adds neural networks only where the maths is blind: how real bacteria respond, and what each batch is hiding.

  • A neural differential equation, trained on GPUs
  • Learns from real growth curves and new lab counts of milk
  • Reads a few cheap tests of the milk at loading
  • Says how sure it is, and is tested on data it never saw
QUESTION 1Does real milk run on one clock?If yes, one quick lab test can replace a whole temperature study. If no, we find exactly where the textbook equations break.
QUESTION 2Which cheap test buys the most accuracy?A dye test, a plate count, pH or a heat test, ranked by how much each one tells us per rupee.
QUESTION 3Do chilled foods share hidden biology?If what the model learns on milk carries over to meat and fish, one monitor can serve the whole cold chain.
